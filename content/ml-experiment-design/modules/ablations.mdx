<Explanation id="intro">

An **ablation study** answers "how much is each piece of my system
actually contributing?" by systematically removing one component at a
time and re-measuring performance. A component whose removal barely
hurts the score isn't pulling its weight — it's a candidate to simplify
away, or a sign something's misconfigured.

```python
def component_contributions(full_score, ablation_scores):
    return {name: round(full_score - score, 4) for name, score in ablation_scores.items()}
```

</Explanation>

<CodeExample id="try-it" language="python" code={`def component_contributions(full_score, ablation_scores):\n    return {name: round(full_score - score, 4) for name, score in ablation_scores.items()}\n\nfull_score = 0.90\n# score of the model with EACH component individually removed\nablations = {"feature_x": 0.85, "feature_y": 0.88, "regularization": 0.90}\nprint(component_contributions(full_score, ablations))\n# {'feature_x': 0.05, 'feature_y': 0.02, 'regularization': 0.0}\n# regularization contributes NOTHING here -- removing it didn't hurt the score at all`} caption="A contribution of exactly 0.0 (or even negative -- removing a component that IMPROVES the score) is a genuinely useful finding, not a bug in the study: it means that piece of complexity isn't earning its keep, or is actively hurting." />
